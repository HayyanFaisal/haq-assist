SCRIPT: Our data plan. We don’t train a model on law; the app looks the law up. The library is 54 acts, 4,716 sections, downloaded only from the five official government portals: Pakistan Code, and the Punjab, Sindh, KP and Balochistan codes. Every PDF passed a provenance check, with its official host, a SHA-256 hash and its title recorded. We compared each act’s own contents list with what we extracted, and got 99.5 percent federal and 98 percent provincial. Codex, a different AI system, checked samples four times, and anything that failed was excluded rather than trusted. Our labelled data is the test sets: 57 short questions and 78 everyday stories, 40 of which Codex wrote and we never saw until the final run. Now the key result, the ablation. A is Gemma alone, from memory: it wrote 77 citations, and not one quote is in the official text. B adds our search but no checks: it still shows 17 citations we can’t back. C is Haq Assist: 121 of 121 citations verifiable, zero unverifiable, and it keeps 41 of B’s 42 correct answers. The checks rejected 28 unsupported steps. One honest note: these are single runs.
