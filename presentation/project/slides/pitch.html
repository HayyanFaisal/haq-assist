In one sentence

Haq Assist finds the Pakistani law that applies to you, explains it in Urdu, shows the excerpt — and says when it can’t

Ali, a student in Islamabad, is picked up at a checkpoint at night.

1

He asks by voice

Spoken Urdu is transcribed locally. He watches it read the question, search the law and check each quote.

2

He gets the law

Art. 10 and CrPC §61: a magistrate within 24 hours of arrest, travel time excluded — quoted, with the PDF page, read aloud in Urdu.

3

It admits limits

“Must I unlock my phone at a checkpoint?” — no verified answer found: “Not found”, see a lawyer.

4

Later, a faulty phone

Draft an ICT complaint (§§2, 8) for review — or, in Punjab, a §28 notice to the manufacturer first.

For step 2: further detention needs a magistrate’s authority; §61 is about arrest without a warrant; the 24-hour rule does not apply to preventive detention (Art. 10(3)). Step 3 is a real test outcome, not a claim that no law exists. Step 4 drafts are for review before use. The voice reply reads only the verified answer.
