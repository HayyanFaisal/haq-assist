SCRIPT: Why not just use ChatGPT? Because an AI answering from memory makes up laws: one model we tested, asked only to reword a question, invented a “Consumer Protection Act 2012”. We could fine-tune a model on our laws, but that needs thousands of checked examples we don’t have, and it still can’t show where an answer came from. So we used RAG, retrieval-augmented generation. It works like an open-book exam: retrieve the relevant law first, then generate the answer only from it. When a law changes, we update the library, not the model. Our addition is a third step that checks every quote. The hard part of RAG is retrieval, finding the right page, so we compared 69 search setups on 49 test questions. A hit means the right section is among the first five results. Keyword search scored 31 percent, because people don’t use the law’s words. Letting the AI rewrite the question in legal words: 71. Searching by meaning with embeddings: 88. Two embedding models plus a reranker, a second model that re-reads the top results and re-orders them: 98 percent. That’s what we built.
