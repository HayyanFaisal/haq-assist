How it works

Five steps from question to answer

StepWhat happens to our stolen-phone questionUnder the hood
1 · Understand“FIR nahi kaat rahe” becomes “police refusing to register a report of theft”; province notedGemma 4 12B, run locally; also writes law-like sentences to search with (HyDE)
2 · Look upSearches the law and keeps the 6 best sections, including CrPC 154 and 22-ATwo embedding models plus a reranker; that province’s laws and federal law
3 · AnswerWrites simple steps in Urdu and English, each quoting one sectionStrict JSON output; temperature 0 to keep answers consistent
4 · CheckEvery quote and number is compared with the official text; a step whose quote doesn’t support it is removedCode-based text matching, then a second AI pass on support; any error means reject
5 · ShowThe answer, its quotes and a link to the government PDF, or “Not found”Web app; Whisper for voice in, OmniVoice for voice out

Everything runs on one PC with one 16 GB graphics card: no cloud AI.

SCRIPT: Here’s our stolen-phone question going through the system. One, understand: Gemma 4, running on our PC, turns “FIR nahi kaat rahe” into “police refusing to register a report of theft”. For long stories it also writes sentences phrased the way a law would say it and searches with those; that’s called HyDE. Two, look up: the embedding models and the reranker search that province’s laws plus federal law, and keep six sections, here including CrPC 154 and 22-A. Three, answer: Gemma writes steps in Urdu and English in a strict format, each quoting one section, at temperature zero to keep answers consistent. Four, the step that makes it safe: check. Our code compares every quote and number with the official text, and a second AI pass confirms the quote really supports the advice. If anything fails, that step is removed. Five, show: the answer, the quotes and the PDF link, or “Not found”.
