Technique

Retrieve the law, then check the supporting excerpts

✕

Plain LLM: answers from memory. Asked only to rephrase a question, Qwen3.5-9B invented a “Consumer Protection Act 2012”.

✕

Fine-tuning: bakes the law into weights that go stale with every amendment, and needs training data we don’t have.

✓

RAG with checks: the library updates without retraining; answers use only retrieved text, and quotes and numbers are checked in code.

+

For stories: the LLM writes statute-style search sentences (rights, remedy, offence: HyDE). Each is searched, and reviewed rights-card law is pinned.

A labelled section in the top 5: 48 of 49 in selection; 46 of 49 in the deployed app, where the 3 misses first ask for the province. All on 1,000-character windows with act and section headings; 69 configurations tested.

Same 49 labelled questions used to select the configuration, region supplied. Models: BAAI/bge-m3, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, bge-reranker-v2-m3.

Why RAG: the library stays current without retraining, and every answer can show its source. In the lecture’s terms this is advanced, modular RAG: rewrite and predict (HyDE) before retrieval, hybrid dense retrieval fused by RRF, a reranker after it, and a reflect step that checks the answer. The chart is a retrieval score, not end-to-end answer quality; that is the next slide’s ablation. The story fix in one example: “remedy for police refusal to register FIR” put CrPC 22-A outside the top 200; worded as a statute would phrase it, 22-A ranks first.
